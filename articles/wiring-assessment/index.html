Wiring Assessment: A Home Love Electric Reference Guide

Updated November 2026

Wiring assessment in Clearwater

Wiring assessment, called an Electrical Wiring Evaluation, is a systematic evaluation of existing residential electrical wiring covering insulation condition, wire gauge, circuit continuity, grounding integrity, voltage ratings, current capacity, visual defect density and code compliance across every branch circuit in the home.

A wiring assessment is a type of Electrical Inspection, which means its findings describe the current state of the system rather than change it. A wiring installation or panel upgrade changes the system; a wiring assessment only measures and reports on it.

Scope and component parts of a wiring assessment

Each part targets a different failure mode, so skipping one leaves a gap in the findings.

  • Visual inspection: Visual inspection identifies physical defects along the wire run, measured as defects per 100 feet of conductor.
  • Load calculation: Load calculation tallies the number of branch circuits and compares the total draw against panel capacity.
  • Thermal imaging scan: Thermal imaging scan detects heat signatures at connections and splices that a visual pass alone cannot find.
  • Insulation evaluation: Insulation evaluation rates each conductor as good, fair or poor and records insulation resistance in ohms.
  • Conductivity check: Conductivity check measures circuit continuity in ohms to confirm an unbroken path from source to load.
  • Electrical testing: Electrical testing verifies voltage at each circuit against its rated value and confirms current capacity in amps.
  • Report generation: Report generation documents every finding in a format a contractor or inspector can act on directly.

Measurable attributes covered in a full wiring assessment

A full wiring assessment produces specific, numeric findings for each attribute. Results are not a simple pass-or-fail summary. The wire gauge range in a typical residential system runs from AWG 14 for 15-amp lighting circuits up to AWG 4/0 for service entrance conductors. Voltage ratings on branch circuits are 120 V for standard outlets and 240 V for appliance circuits. Current capacity varies by gauge: AWG 14 carries 15 amps, AWG 12 carries 20 amps, and AWG 10 carries 30 amps. Circuit continuity readings above a fraction of an ohm on a low-resistance test point to a loose connection or a corroded splice.

Grounding integrity is recorded as a pass or fail against the measured ground-to-neutral voltage at each circuit. Grounding systems that fail this check are flagged for immediate correction because an open ground removes the fault-current path that protects occupants. Visual defect density, expressed as defects per 100 feet, gives a normalized comparison across circuits of different lengths so the worst runs are prioritized first.

Wiring assessment checklist: what the report covers

A wiring assessment checklist organizes findings into four categories: conductor condition, circuit performance, grounding status, and code observations. Each category is distinct. Requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the local building authority.

The completed report distinguishes a wiring assessment from a wiring repair or system upgrade: the assessment describes what exists, while a repair or upgrade changes it. That distinction matters when a homeowner needs documentation for an insurance carrier, a real estate transaction or a permit application, because the assessment report is an independent record of system condition at a specific point in time.

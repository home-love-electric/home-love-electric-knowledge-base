## Federal Pacific Panel in Clearwater

Federal Pacific Panel, called an FPE panel, is a type of electrical panel manufactured and installed in residential homes from the 1950s through the 1980s, built around Stab-Lok circuit breakers, rated from 60 to 200 amperes, and fitted with 12 to 42 breaker slots depending on the model. A Federal Pacific Panel is a type of [main breaker panel](https://home-love-electric.mintlify.site/main-breaker-panel), meaning it distributes power from the utility feed to every circuit in the house through a central enclosure.

### Parts and construction of a Federal Pacific Panel

The enclosure of a Federal Pacific Panel houses the main breaker in the upper section, the bus bar, and individual circuit breakers arranged in two columns. Federal Pacific Panel construction centers on the Stab-Lok breaker. This snap-in design clips onto an aluminum or copper bus bar running down the center of the enclosure. Bus bars carry current from the main breaker to each slot. Breaker count ranges from 12 slots in smaller 60-ampere models to 42 slots in larger 200-ampere configurations. The Stab-Lok breaker is the component most associated with the Federal Pacific Panel fire hazard, because its retention mechanism can fail to hold contact under load.

A Federal Pioneer Panel is the Canadian-market name for the same product line, sharing the Stab-Lok design and the same documented failure characteristics. Both names refer to panels built on identical internal architecture. An electrician inspecting either variant looks at the same components: the main breaker, the bus bar, the individual Stab-Lok breakers, and the enclosure condition. ### Measurable attributes and ratings

Federal Pacific Panel amperage ratings ran from 60A in older single-family homes to 200A in larger residential installations. Breaker slot counts ranged from 12 to 42, with 24-slot and 30-slot configurations being the most common in mid-century construction. Voltage rating matched the standard North American residential supply of 120/240 volts. Bus bar material varied by production era, with earlier units using copper and later production shifting to aluminum. These specifications place the FPE panel within the same general class as a [load center](https://home-love-electric.mintlify.site/load-center), though modern load centers use breaker designs with far tighter retention tolerances than the Stab-Lok.

### Federal Pacific Panel fire risk and failure under load

Federal Pacific Panel fire risk stems from a documented high failure rate under load conditions. The Stab-Lok breaker is designed to trip and interrupt current when a circuit is overloaded. Testing has shown that a significant portion of these breakers fail to trip, allowing wiring to overheat. Overheated wiring causes fires. The failure is not always visible from outside the panel, because the breaker handle may appear in the tripped position while the internal contacts remain closed and current continues to flow.

The Federal Pacific Panel fire hazard is compounded by age. Panels manufactured in the 1950s through 1980s are now 40 to 70 years old, and the plastic components, spring mechanisms, and bus bar connections all degrade over that span. A breaker that passed its original factory test may no longer perform reliably after decades of thermal cycling. This is why home inspectors flag FPE panels as a safety concern even when the panel appears undamaged. ### Federal Pacific Panel relates to the broader electrical system

Federal Pacific Panel sits at the distribution point between the utility meter and every branch circuit in the home. Power enters through the service entrance conductors, passes through the main breaker, and splits across the bus bar to each individual Stab-Lok breaker. Each breaker feeds one circuit, whether that circuit serves outlets, lighting, an air conditioner, or a water heater. A [subpanel](https://home-love-electric.mintlify.site/subpanel) may be downstream of the main FPE panel in larger homes, extending circuits to a garage, workshop, or addition, though a subpanel fed from a failing main panel inherits the same upstream risk. The FPE panel contrasts directly with modern alternatives. A Square D QO Panel, a Siemens panel, or any other modern circuit breaker panel uses breaker designs with tested and certified trip mechanisms, updated bus bar tolerances, and current UL listing. An upgraded electrical panel replaces the entire enclosure along with its breakers, eliminating the Stab-Lok design entirely. A safe electrical panel in a modern home trips reliably at rated amperage. A performance standard the Federal Pacific Panel cannot consistently meet.

Federal Pacific Panel upgrade work in Clearwater involves pulling a permit, disconnecting the utility feed, removing the existing enclosure and all Stab-Lok breakers, and installing a new panel with a current-listed main breaker and branch breakers. Amperage matters. The replacement panel's amperage depends on the home's current load and any planned additions such as an electric vehicle charger or additional air conditioning circuits.

## Main service panel in Clearwater

The main service panel, called an Electrical Panel or Breaker Panel, is the central distribution point where utility electricity enters a building and splits into individual branch circuits. Those circuits power lights, outlets, appliances, and major systems throughout the structure. Every circuit in the home traces back to it. It sits at the highest level of a building's electrical hierarchy. ### Parts that make up a main service panel

A main service panel is built from four core components: circuit breakers, bus bars, grounding wires and a main disconnect switch. Circuit breakers are the individual protective devices that interrupt current when a circuit is overloaded. Bus bars are the metal bars that carry current from the main disconnect to each breaker. Grounding wires connect the panel to the earth, giving fault current a safe return path. The main disconnect switch cuts power to the entire panel in a single action. Requirements can vary and should be confirmed with the local building authority before any panel work begins.

### Measurable attributes of a main service panel

A main service panel carries specific, measurable ratings that determine what a home can safely power. Amperage Rating runs from 100A on older homes up to 400A on large modern ones, with 200A being the most common residential standard today. Voltage is 120/240V split-phase, meaning the panel delivers 120V to standard outlets and 240V to heavy loads like air conditioners and electric water heaters. Breaker Capacity ranges from 20 to 42 circuits depending on the panel's physical size, and a panel that has run out of open slots needs either a [main breaker panel](https://home-love-electric.mintlify.site/main-breaker-panel) upgrade or the addition of a subpanel to handle new circuits. Panel dimensions vary by manufacturer and amperage class, but a typical 200A residential enclosure measures roughly 14 by 32 inches. This size affects rough-in clearance requirements during installation. ### How the main service panel relates to the broader electrical system

The main service panel connects directly to the meter socket on the exterior of the building, which is where the utility company measures consumption. The meter socket is a distinct device. It is not a panel and carries no breakers, so the two are separate points in the service entrance sequence. From the meter socket, service entrance conductors run into the Service Entrance Panel (SEP), which is the formal name for the main panel when it contains the main disconnect. A [subpanel](https://home-love-electric.mintlify.site/subpanel) is a downstream distribution board fed by a circuit from the main panel; it handles a specific area or structure but does not receive power directly from the utility. A [load center](https://home-love-electric.mintlify.site/load-center) is the manufacturer's term for the same enclosure type, used interchangeably with Breaker Panel in product specifications. ### Common problems and signs a main service panel needs attention

The main service panel shows recognizable signs when it is failing or undersized. Breakers that trip repeatedly under normal loads indicate the panel is drawing more current than its rating supports. Scorch marks or a burning smell near the enclosure point to arcing and create a fire risk requiring immediate inspection. Rust or moisture inside the cabinet corrodes bus bars and loosens connections over time. Panels rated below 100A struggle to support modern appliances, EV chargers, and air conditioning simultaneously. That makes main service panel replacement the practical solution rather than repeated breaker resets. Federal Pacific and Zinsco panels from earlier decades are known to have breakers that fail to trip on overload, and electricians typically recommend replacing those enclosures outright. A buzzing or crackling sound from the panel is never normal.
